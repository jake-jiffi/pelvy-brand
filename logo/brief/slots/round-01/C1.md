# Concept C1: Symbolic

Brand name, set exactly: Pelvy
What it is: For pelvic health clinicians whose patients lose the thread between appointments, Pelvy is the between-appointments companion that puts the clinician's own advice, exercises and reminders on the patient's phone, because it was built by a practising pelvic health physiotherapist from what patients and clinicians asked for. Unlike a handout or a Kegel timer, it counts the patient through the whole program and keeps the advice where they will find it. Said plainly: what you tell your patients in the room, still with them at home.
Who sees it: Pelvic health clinicians
The brand's own hand, which this mark has to sit beside: The chosen direction: Corporate trust. Vary the idea, not the hand.

The question this concept answers: What thing does this business actually put in front of people?

## Your constraints, which are not negotiable

Architecture: Lockup, wordmark plus symbol.
Typographic register: Humanist sans. Set the wordmark in one of: Cabin, Nunito Sans, Josefin Sans, Julius Sans One, Marcellus.
Symbol approach: literal. Depict the actual thing, specifically. Not "a leaf" but the leaf of the plant this business grows.
What this signals: Professional but warm.
Where it sits: Inside the healthcare convention, which reads as competence.
The risk you are carrying: Reads as safe when the brief wanted a position. Counter it: Push the case or the weight further than feels comfortable.

Build the symbol-only FIRST, at 16 pixels, and let it drive the rest.

## Refused outright

- swoosh
- letter with an angled cut corner
- monogram in a heavy ring
- shield with the year founded
- filigree monogram
- shield
- infinity loop
- handshake
- medical cross
- caduceus
- heartbeat line
- neural network of circles and lines
- brain with circuit traces
- triangle with an eye
- sparkle or four-pointed star
- abstract globe of curved lines

Also refused: any gradient (it fails five of the ten application contexts), any live text
element, any raster image, more than two ideas in one mark.
